A hospital pharmacy runs a shared electronic prescription record that several pharmacists update as they dispense medication. To coordinate, a pharmacist must first acquire a time-limited lease on the record; the lease names one owner and carries an expiry time measured against a shared clock. When a pharmacist acquires the lease it snapshots the record's current version number, and it may commit an update only while its lease is still valid and only if the record's version still matches the snapshot it took. Committing an update advances the record version by one and remembers which snapshot the write was based on. A pharmacist can crash silently at any point: it stops acting but does NOT release its lease, so the lease is reclaimed only once it expires against the clock and another pharmacist takes over. The clock ticks forward, leases expire, a non-crashed owner may also release its lease explicitly, and a crashed pharmacist may later revive and resume work. Because a crashed owner's stale lease can be taken over after expiry, the danger is that a slow or revived writer commits an update based on a version that a newer owner has already overwritten, silently losing that newer update. The version-match guard on commit is what prevents this.
SAFETY PROPERTY: Every committed update to the prescription record is based on the immediately preceding version, so no update is ever overwritten and lost.
