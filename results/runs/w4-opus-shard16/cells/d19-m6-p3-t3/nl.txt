A library runs an online reservation system for its physical book copies. A patron reserves a specific copy by taking a time-limited lease on it: the lease names the patron and carries an expiry measured against a shared clock. A patron holds at most one copy at a time on the patron side, and the system grants a reservation on a copy only if no other patron currently holds a still-valid lease on that same copy. Patrons can be slow: a patron may take its time before reserving or releasing, but no patron fails outright. The shared clock ticks forward, and once a patron's lease expires it no longer counts as a valid hold, which frees the copy so another patron may reserve it. A patron may also release a copy explicitly before its lease expires. Because leases expire and slow patrons may act late, the reservation grant must always check that a copy is not already validly held. The essential guarantee is that the same copy is never simultaneously held under two valid leases by two different patrons.
SAFETY PROPERTY: No two distinct patrons ever hold valid leases on the same book copy at the same time.
