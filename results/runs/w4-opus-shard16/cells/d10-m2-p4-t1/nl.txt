A subway signalling system controls entry into a shared central interlocking that can physically hold only a bounded number of trains at once. Before a train may enter, it must obtain movement authority through a two-phase commit across the guarding signals: the train opens a request and, in the first phase, each guarding signal independently votes yes that it is clear. Because the signalling bus reorders messages, the signals' votes may be recorded in any order, not the order they were sent. In the second phase the train may commit and enter only once every guarding signal has voted yes and the interlocking still has room; committing brings the train in and raises the occupancy by one. If authority cannot be completed the request is aborted and the train returns to idle to try again later. A train that has entered later exits, lowering the occupancy by one, and afterwards its slot is recycled so it may request again. However votes are reordered and however requests interleave, the number of trains inside the interlocking must never exceed its fixed capacity.
SAFETY PROPERTY: The number of trains inside the central interlocking never exceeds its fixed capacity.
