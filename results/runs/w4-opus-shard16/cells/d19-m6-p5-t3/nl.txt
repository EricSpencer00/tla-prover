A library runs a deaccession station where worn book copies are permanently archived out of circulation, an irreversible action: once a copy is archived it cannot be brought back. To use the station a staff member takes a time-limited lease naming itself, with an expiry measured against a shared clock; the lease is granted only when it is free or the previous lease has expired. Only a staff member holding a still-valid lease may archive a copy, and only a copy that is still available; archiving marks the copy archived and records that a deaccession has occurred for it. Staff members can be slow, taking their time between acquiring the lease and archiving or releasing, but none fails outright. The shared clock advances and leases expire, freeing the station for another staff member; a holder may also release the station explicitly. Because the station is single and archiving requires a valid lease on an available copy, the irreversible archiving of any given copy happens at most one time.
SAFETY PROPERTY: No book copy is ever archived more than once.
