A car-wash conveyor line has one shared high-pressure sprayer that only a single bay controller may drive at a time. Access is arbitrated by a compare-and-swap register that stores the current owner together with a rotating tag. A controller that wants the sprayer first signals interest and, in doing so, snapshots the register's current tag. It then attempts a compare-and-swap: the swap succeeds only if the register is still free and its tag still equals the snapshot the controller took, at which point the controller becomes the owner and starts spraying. If either the register is no longer free or the tag has moved, the compare-and-swap fails and the controller backs off to idle to try again later; controllers can be slow to retry but none fails outright. When the owner finishes spraying it frees the register and advances the tag so stale attempts cannot succeed. Because the single register admits only one successful swap at a time, the sprayer is driven by at most one controller. The essential guarantee is mutual exclusion on the sprayer.
SAFETY PROPERTY: At no reachable state are two distinct controllers driving the shared sprayer simultaneously.
