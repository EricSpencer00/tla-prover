A hospital pharmacy has one automated narcotics dispensing cabinet that must be operated by a single controller node at a time. The controller nodes run a leader election: whenever there is no leader, any node may stand and become the leader for a new term, and a leader may fail, vacating leadership so a failover election picks another. A node signals interest by moving from idle to waiting. Only the current leader may open the cabinet and begin dispensing, and it may do so only when no other node is dispensing and the pharmacist administrator is not in control. When it finishes it returns to idle. A privileged pharmacist administrator holds an override: the administrator may seize the cabinet at any moment, which forces any dispensing node back to idle, revokes the current leadership, and takes exclusive control. The administrator later steps out and normal leader-driven dispensing resumes. Across elections, failovers, and administrator overrides, the cabinet must never be operated by two parties at once.
SAFETY PROPERTY: At most one party operates the dispensing cabinet at any time: never two nodes together, and never a node while the administrator is in control.
