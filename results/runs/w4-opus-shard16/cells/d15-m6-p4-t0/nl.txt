A stock exchange runs an opening auction whose order book can hold only a bounded number of resting orders at once. The right to submit into the book is granted by a time-limited lease: a market-maker takes a lease naming itself, with an expiry measured against a shared clock, and the lease is granted only when it is free or the previous one has expired. While holding a still-valid lease a market-maker may submit a new order into an open book slot, but only if the book is not already at capacity, and it may cancel an order it recognises, freeing that slot. A market-maker can crash silently at any time: it stops acting but does not release its lease, so the lease is reclaimed only once it expires against the advancing clock, after which another market-maker can take over; a crashed market-maker may later recover. The clock ticks forward and leases expire, and a non-crashed holder may also release its lease explicitly. Through lease takeovers, crashes, and recoveries, the number of resting orders in the book must never exceed its fixed capacity.
SAFETY PROPERTY: The number of resting orders in the opening-auction book never exceeds its fixed capacity.
