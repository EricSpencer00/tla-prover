A hospital operating-room scheduler lets surgeons book individual time slots on a shared theatre. Booking uses two-level hierarchical locking: a surgeon must first take the coarse day-lock on the theatre's schedule, and only while holding it may it claim individual fine-grained time slots. Because the coarse lock has a single holder, bookings are serialised. While holding the coarse lock a surgeon may claim a slot only if no other surgeon already holds that slot, adding it to the surgeon's own set of claimed slots; the surgeon may also cancel a slot it holds, releasing it. When done the surgeon releases the coarse lock so another surgeon may book. A surgeon can crash silently at any point: it stops acting but does not release the coarse lock, so the lock is reclaimed by the scheduler once it detects the crash, and the crashed surgeon may later recover. Its already-claimed slots remain booked to it. Across serialised booking, crashes, lock reclamation, and recovery, no single time slot is ever booked to two different surgeons at once.
SAFETY PROPERTY: No time slot is ever held by two different surgeons at the same time.
