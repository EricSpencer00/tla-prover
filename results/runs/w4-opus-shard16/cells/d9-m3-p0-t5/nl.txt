A regional power grid uses a cluster of controller nodes to drive one shared load-shedding breaker, which is the critical resource: at most one actor may be actuating that breaker at any instant. The nodes run a leader election: whenever there is no leader, any node may stand for election and, on winning, becomes the leader for a new term. Only the current leader may begin actuating the breaker, and it may do so only when no one else is actuating it. A leader can fail at any time; failure clears its actuation and vacates the leadership so a failover election can pick a new leader for a higher term. A privileged grid administrator holds an override: the administrator may seize the breaker at any moment, which forcibly stops whatever node is actuating it, revokes the current leadership, and hands actuation to the administrator alone. The administrator later releases the breaker, and normal leader-driven operation resumes. Throughout all of this the breaker must never be driven by two actors at once, whether two nodes, or a node and the administrator.
SAFETY PROPERTY: At no reachable state are two distinct actors actuating the shared breaker simultaneously.
