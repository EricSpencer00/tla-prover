A container-ship crane yard loads containers into a shared holding bay that has a fixed capacity. To place a container safely a crane uses two-level hierarchical locking: it must first take the coarse bay-lock, and only while holding it may it take a fine stack-lock on the particular stack it wants to build. While holding a stack's fine lock a crane may load a container onto that stack, which raises the bay occupancy by one, but only if the bay still has room; it may also unload a container, lowering occupancy by one. A crane releases the fine stack-lock and then the coarse bay-lock so another crane can work. A privileged yard administrator holds an override: the administrator may seize the yard at any moment, which drops all crane locks and takes exclusive control, and while in control the administrator may drain the bay down to empty in an emergency. The administrator later releases control and normal locked loading resumes. Through hierarchical locking, unloads, administrator seizes, and emergency drains, the number of containers in the bay must never exceed its fixed capacity.
SAFETY PROPERTY: The number of containers in the holding bay never exceeds its fixed capacity.
