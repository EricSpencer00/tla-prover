A wildfire surveillance operation runs a swarm of drones that shuttle between the ground depot and the fire perimeter; every drone in the fleet is always in exactly one of those two places, so the fleet total is conserved. The number of drones allowed on the perimeter at once is a station capacity that changes at runtime: operations can raise it when conditions are calm or lower it when winds pick up, though never below the number already deployed. Launch and recall are coordinated through a compare-and-swap register holding the current perimeter count together with a rotating stamp. A pilot preparing a launch or a recall first snapshots the register's count and stamp. A launch commits only if the register still matches the snapshot, the perimeter is below capacity, and a drone is present at the depot; it moves one drone from depot to perimeter and advances the stamp. A recall commits symmetrically, moving one drone from perimeter back to depot when the register still matches and a drone is out, likewise advancing the stamp. If the register has moved since the snapshot the attempt fails and the pilot must re-snapshot before retrying. Because every transfer is a matched decrement in one place and increment in the other, drones are never created or destroyed: depot plus perimeter always equals the fleet size.
SAFETY PROPERTY: The number of drones at the depot plus the number on the perimeter always equals the fixed fleet total, so no drone is ever created or destroyed.
