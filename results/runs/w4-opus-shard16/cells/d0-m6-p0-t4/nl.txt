A hospital operating-room suite shares one surgical robot that is the critical resource: at most one surgical team may hold it at any instant. A team obtains the robot by taking a time-limited lease with an expiry measured against a shared clock, and the scheduler grants the lease only when the robot is in service and no other team currently holds a still-valid lease. Staffing capacity changes at runtime: teams are activated and stood down as shifts change, and only an active team may be granted the robot, while a team that is stood down must not be holding it. The shared clock advances, and once a team's lease expires the team no longer counts as holding the robot, freeing it for another team; a team may also release the robot explicitly. The robot can be taken out of service for maintenance at any time, which forcibly clears any current hold, and later returned to service. Whatever the sequence of grants, expiries, staffing changes, and maintenance, the robot must never be validly held by two different teams at once, and must never be held while it is out of service.
SAFETY PROPERTY: At every reachable state at most one team validly holds the surgical robot, and no team holds it while it is out of service.
